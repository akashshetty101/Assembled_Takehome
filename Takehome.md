### Context

Assembled is a platform for contact center operations. Customer support agents work scheduled shifts answering tickets across queues (e.g., “refunds”, "billing", "tier 2 support"). Operations managers oversee hundreds (sometimes thousands) of agents and need to react quickly when things go wrong during the day. This is especially acute if a queue is backing up, an agent went on an unscheduled break, or volume is way higher than forecasted.A glossary of terms you'll see in the data:**Queue.** A logical bucket of issues, usually by topic or skill. Each queue has a predefined SLA e.g., "all refund tickets will be answered within 2 hours" or “all urgent requests will be answered within 5 minutes”**SLA.** The deadline a queue promises for answering a ticket (sla\_target\_sec). Each snapshot reports the oldest ticket's wait time (longest\_wait\_sec): when that exceeds the deadline, the SLA is breached; as it nears the deadline, the SLA is at risk.**Agent state.** What an agent is currently doing: available, on\_call, on\_break, in\_meeting, offline, etc.**Adherence.** Whether an agent is doing what they're scheduled to be doing right now. (Scheduled to be available but currently in a meeting = out of adherence.)**Forecast.** A prediction of how many issues a queue will see in some upcoming window.That is all the domain knowledge you need.

### The problem

Build an **intraday notification system**.Support teams need to know when things are happening during the day, and different people on the team care about different things. An agent might want a heads-up when they've drifted out of adherence so they can get back on track. A team lead might want to know the moment a queue starts breaching SLA. A head of support might only want a summary unless something is genuinely on fire.The heart of the system is **rule configuration**. End users should be able to set up the notifications that make sense for them (e.g. "notify me when I've been out of adherence for more than ten minutes," "tell me when the billing queue has more than 20 tickets waiting," "ping me if any of my agents has been on a single call for over 45 minutes”). Your job is to figure out what that experience looks like, how rules get evaluated against a live stream of events, and how notifications reach the right person at the right time without becoming noise.Who _exactly_ you build for and what that experience looks like is part of the product decision. Agents, team leads, heads of support: pick the audiences that make the most sense and justify it.
